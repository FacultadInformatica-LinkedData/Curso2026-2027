Dataset Requirements: Sports facilities of Madrid

Group 08. We selected three datasets about sports places in Madrid, all published by the Madrid City Council open data portal. They form one topic: where people can practise sport in Madrid.

2. Requirements

R1. Data are in one of the selected smart city domains

The datasets describe urban sports and leisure facilities (pitches, courts, sports centres, pools) and the sports entities that use them. They belong to the leisure/sport and urban facilities domain of a smart city, and relate to citizens' health and well-being.

R2. Data are available as a CSV file

All three datasets are downloadable as CSV and are stored in the csv directory. The files are semicolon-separated and encoded in ISO-8859-1. Conversion to UTF-8 will be needed before RDF generation.

R3. Data have an open licence

All three datasets are published under Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 International (CC BY 4.0). It allows copying, adapting and republishing, including in RDF form, as long as the original source is credited. We will credit the Ayuntamiento de Madrid in the published data.

R4. Data can be easily linked with generic real-world entities

The three datasets share the same 32 columns. Entities that can be linked:

Other sources that could be linked with ours: Madrid metro and bus stops (to describe transport access to a facility) and other open datasets on the same portal, such as municipal parks or BiciMAD stations. The three datasets can also be linked with each other: a swimming pool is often located in a sports centre.

R5. Documentation exists (optional)

Each dataset has a description page on the Madrid open data portal with the update frequency, the unit that publishes it and the licence. The column names are mostly self-explanatory, but we did not find an explanation for some codes, for example what the values 0, 1, 2 and 3 mean in ACCESIBILIDAD.

R6. Data can come from multiple data sources (optional)

All three datasets come from the same portal and the same publisher (Ayuntamiento de Madrid), so on their own they are one source, not several. The other sources come when we link the data: Wikidata, DBpedia and GeoNames for districts, neighbourhoods and the city, and possibly Madrid transport stops.
